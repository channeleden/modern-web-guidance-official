Good to know

Clear from
the start.

Does accessible mean plain?

It means making deliberate choices. Expressive type, strong color, thoughtful layouts, and satisfying interactions can sit alongside clear reading, useful feedback, and familiar controls.

The experience should make sense with a mouse, a keyboard, a touch screen, or assistive technology.

Can you improve my existing website?

Yes. The right starting point may be a focused improvement rather than a full rebuild. Share the parts people struggle with, what your site runs on, and what you want to preserve.

I’ll use that context to discuss a sensible scope. Some changes depend on the access and flexibility your current platform provides.

What does an accessibility target mean?

WCAG describes testable accessibility requirements. I work toward the agreed target and document the pages, interactions, checks, and any gaps in scope.

A target is not a certification or a guarantee of legal compliance. Real review includes keyboard use, zoom and reflow, and assistive technology, with disabled-user feedback where it is included in the project.

What should I have ready?

An idea is enough to start a brief: who your site is for, what they need to do, and what is getting in the way. An existing website, content, or a timeline can help, but you do not need a finished specification.

About accessibility, privacy, and this page

Built with access in mind.

This page uses semantic headings, native controls, visible keyboard focus, and layouts designed to reflow. It respects reduced-motion and forced-color preferences, leaves browser zoom available, and keeps essential information out of hover-only effects.

The design target is WCAG 2.2 AA, with stronger focus indicators and generous control targets. This is a working reference page, not a certified or fully audited conformance claim. Browser, assistive-technology, and disabled-user review are still needed.

Your notes stay with you.

The brief tool does not send requests, store notes in site storage, or load analytics. With JavaScript, it prepares text on this page and lets you explicitly copy or download it. Without JavaScript, you can use the native worksheet or download a text template to fill in your own editor.

In compatible experimental browsers, WebMCP form annotations let a browser agent help fill the same visible form. You review it before preparing the brief. Experimental support is optional; ordinary controls remain available.
